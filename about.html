About

Who's behind the investigations, and what this site is trying to do.

Hi, I'm [Your Name].

I'm a digital forensics and incident response analyst. My day-to-day is reconstructing what happened on a compromised host or network from whatever evidence survives — event logs, memory images, disk artifacts, mobile backups, and mail headers — and turning that into a timeline someone else can act on.

I started this site because most public DFIR writeups either over-simplify into a Q&A format ("what tool answers what question") or skip straight to conclusions. I wanted a place to show the actual investigative narrative: the dead ends, the pivot from one artifact to the next, and the reasoning that connects them — alongside the diagrams and tables I'd actually use in a real case report.

Why DFIR matters: attribution, containment, and legal outcomes all rest on the quality of the evidence chain behind them. A conclusion is only as strong as the artifacts and methodology that produced it — so that's what I try to document here, not just the headline finding.

Focus areas

Windows endpoint forensics, Linux server compromise, macOS/mobile artifact analysis, memory forensics, and email-based intrusion analysis (BEC, phishing infrastructure).
